03 · Positioning

If a brand gave you one minute.

These answers become the opening of the site. They are the most demanding questions here and the most important. Write them as you would say them aloud.

"Beauty influencer" does not count. Try this shape: I help ______ so that ______.
Two audiences here: the women watching, and the brands paying. Answer for both if they differ.
If you cannot name what is wrong for them, you are not selling, you are announcing. For the woman watching: what is she getting wrong before your video? For the brand: what is failing in their marketing before you make the reel?
Nobody wants to join a full stop. What are you building towards this year?
Three rules for all three: say "I" not "we", keep it to one or two sentences, and finish on a number.
A follower who learned something. A brand whose product moved. Anyone.
DMs answered, comments replied to, women who asked what product you used and got a real answer. The dashboard cannot see any of this and it is often the truest number you have.

Brief to posted in how many days. Filming to edit in how many hours.
Save rate, share rate, watch time, repeat viewers, comments asking for the product name. These sit in your insights and almost no creator quotes them.
You have a reputation whether you chose it or not. Somebody is describing you in one sentence right now. Best guess at that sentence.
A smaller pond where you are known beats an ocean where you are invisible. "Graphic designer" becomes "I design packaging for food brands abroad". What is yours? Hijabi everyday beauty? 07 · Rates

Rates.

You do not currently have a rate card, and that is the single largest thing costing you money. A brand with no figure in front of it will either open low or go quiet. Work through the four questions below and the number follows from them.

If your reel does 100K views for a product that costs them $8 to send, what would that reach have cost them in ads? That is the ceiling.
Filming, lighting, editing, the takes that failed, the years learning. Never price below this.
Not so you can undercut. So you know the room.
Honest answer. It changes how the page is written.
A range is fine. Per reel, per story set, per usage month.
Free that pays you back buys a testimonial, a case study, or a door you cannot open yet. Free that drains you is a favour for someone who could have paid. If you cannot name what you are buying, the answer is no. Which gifted jobs have actually bought you something, and which just cost you a day?
A logo is 500 rupees to a street cart and five lakh to a bank. The work did not change, the room did. Should your rate move with the size of the brand, and roughly how?
Answering first is how creators get anchored low. A holding line that asks about deliverables, usage and timing before naming a number is usually worth real money. I can write you one, but tell me how you would rather handle it.
Shown filters out time wasters. Hidden keeps you flexible. Both are valid.

08 · The written approach

The message you send first.

Waiting to be found is slower than reaching out. Four lines is the whole message, and the only measure of success is that they reply at all.

Not "I love your products". Something specific: a launch, a shade range, a market they just entered. Pick a brand you actually want and write the line for them.
One document. Every reel that performed, every brand comment, every message saying thank you, each with a date and a number. Most creators throw this away weekly and then have nothing to show when it is time to raise a rate. If you have one, send it. If not, start it this week.

09 · Restrictions

Restrictions.

Stating clearly what you decline builds trust with the brands you do want.

In the US the FTC requires it, and an undisclosed post is a legal problem for the brand, not just for you. Brands increasingly check. Saying you disclose properly is a selling point.
