Part 01 · Package your value

Turn what you do into something people can repeat.

The deck's first exercise. Nobody buys skills, they buy outcomes, and the person describing you when you are not in the room can only repeat what you gave them.

Job title alone is banned. "Beauty influencer" does not count. Name one thing you fixed, built, taught or saved. Shape: I help ______ so that ______.

Every selling sentence has four parts. These are the four.

"Everyone who wears makeup" means nobody. Specific is not small, specific is magnetic. Say "women who get ready in five minutes" and every one of them leans forward.
If you cannot name their pain you are not selling, you are announcing. What is she getting wrong, or afraid of, before she watches you?

Nobody can copy where you started.

Dhoni never hid Ranchi, it became the brand. Your competitor can copy your ring light and your camera. She cannot copy where you began. Do not hide it.
Only if you want it on the page. Modest beauty is a serious market and it is part of your positioning, but it is entirely your call.

You already have numbers. Count them.

DMs answered, comments replied to, women who asked what product you used and got a real answer. The dashboard cannot see any of this.
Product value gifted to you, affiliate sales through your codes, anything a brand earned because of a post. Rough is fine.
Save rate, share rate, watch time, repeat viewers, comments asking for the product name. These sit in your insights and almost no creator quotes them.

Three PAR bullets. Problem, action, result. The rules: say "I" not "we", one or two sentences each, and finish on a number.

Part 02 · The art of the pitch

Four pitches, one formula.

The deck gives four lengths for four different jobs. Fifteen seconds so they can repeat it to somebody else. Sixty so they ask a follow up question. Three minutes for the real conversation. And the written one, where success is that they reply at all.

This is the part that makes people want to help you. Nobody wants to join a full stop.

The written one. Four lines is the whole message.

Not "I love your products". A launch, a shade range, a market they just entered. Pick a brand you actually want and write it for them.
Exercise 3 in the deck. The version you write and the version that survives your own ear are never the same.

Part 03 · A brand that works while you sleep

What they say about you when you are not there.

Your brand is not a logo or a filter. It is the single sentence somebody is using to describe you right now, which you have never heard, and which is deciding your career.

A smaller pond where you are known beats an ocean where you are invisible. "Graphic designer" becomes "I design packaging for food brands abroad". Hijabi everyday beauty? Two product looks for women with no time? Drugstore that looks expensive? Narrow it until it feels almost too narrow, then stop.

Four things must exist. Tell me what you have and what is missing.

They will Google you. Right now there is no website, so the answer is Instagram or nothing.
Brand comments, DMs, screenshots. You throw away gold every week.
If beauty brands scout on TikTok and your TikTok has 85 followers, that is a choice being made for you. Be honest about which platforms you can realistically keep alive.

Three content formats the deck says always travel.

"I ruined my skin barrier doing X. Here is what I do instead." Nobody scrolls past a confession.
This is the one that gets forwarded. It is also, looking at your reels, already what you are best at.
"Three products, four minutes, and it lasted eleven hours." A number stops the thumb.
The deck is blunt that a network is built before you need it. Other creators, small brands, a shop owner. Sifarish is a favour, a network is not.

Part 04 · Turn experience into offers

A brand enquiry is a sales call.

They are not testing you, they are reducing their risk. Every question a brand asks is really the same question: will this person make me look good, or make me look stupid?

I searched your whole grid, your reels and your tagged tab and could not find it. The link, the clinic name, or their Instagram handle. Any of the three. This is the most valuable single thing in this form, because it is the one piece of proof that you have been hired and delivered rather than only gifted.
All three are respectable. I just have to describe it accurately.

What a brand can actually book.

Reel, story set, TikTok, photos they can run in their own ads, unboxing, tutorial, event coverage.
Often worth more than the post itself. Yes, no, or yes for extra.

Something worth checking with you. Both of your biggest reels name no brand at all. The 3.6M was two products in five minutes. The 1M was a mascara hack. Your viral formula is technique, not product review, and your branded posts sit around 3K.

"Your following is small." "We only work with 50K plus." "We can only offer product." Write the ones you actually get.
The deck's note has three lines: thank them for one real thing, reinforce the fit, and add one new idea. Then one follow up after a week, and stop.

Part 05 · Name your price

Not giving value. Pricing it.

The deck is blunt here: giving value away is not generosity, it is a slow trap. Endless free work drains you and whispers that your time is worthless. You do not currently have a rate card, and that is the single largest thing costing you money.

Free that pays you back buys a testimonial, a case study, or a door you cannot open yet. Free that drains you is a favour for someone who could have paid. If you cannot name what you are buying, the answer is no. Which gifted jobs actually bought you something, and which just cost you a day?

Four questions set any price you will ever quote.

If your reel does 100K views for a product that cost them $8 to post, what would that reach have cost in paid ads? Aim toward that number, never below it.
Filming, lighting, editing, the takes that failed, the years of learning. Never price beneath this.
A reference point only. Never let the cheapest rival set your number.
The freedom to say no is the quiet force that lifts your price. Honest answer, it changes how the page is written.
A range is fine. Per reel, per story set, per month of usage.
A logo is 500 rupees to a street cart and five lakh to a bank. The work did not change, the room did. Should your rate move with the size of the brand, and roughly how?
Quote after a win, not before. Ask while the result is fresh, never after they have cooled.
Answering first is how creators get anchored low. The deck's version of this question costs South Asians more money than any other sentence in their careers. I can write you a holding line, but tell me how you would rather handle it.
Shown filters out time wasters. Hidden keeps you flexible. Both are valid.

Never sell what you cannot deliver.

The FTC requires it in the US and an undisclosed post is a legal problem for the brand, not only for you. Brands check. Saying you disclose properly is a selling point.

Part 06 · What I still need

Material and practical.

Professional dashboard, then Insights, then Total followers. Audience age, gender, top countries and top cities. A media kit without these gets skipped. This is the most important item on this list.
Cover the names. Nothing gets published with a name or handle without that person's permission.
Your grid images are screen quality. Originals off your phone are far better.
Your comments arrive in Arabic, Urdu, Turkish, Russian, Spanish, French and Indonesian. That is unusual at any follower count and it is a commercial asset.
